# Ethical, professional and regulatory constraints on Jude (AI courtroom copilot for Indonesian criminal defense)

Current as of 2026-11-01. Researched via web search/fetch on 2026-11-01. Items marked **[unverified this session]** come from well-known primary sources whose URLs are recorded but whose pages were not re-fetched in this session. Verify article numbers against official texts (JDIH MA, peraturan.bpk.go.id, eur-lex) before relying on them.

## Indonesia: personal data protection (UU 27/2022 PDP, PP 33/2026, UU ITE)

### Takeaway
UU PDP has been fully binding since 17 Oct 2024. Its implementing regulation, PP 33/2026, was promulgated on 16 Jul 2026 and takes effect on 16 Jan 2027. The PDP Agency (Lembaga PDP) is still not operational. Jude processes "specific" personal data (criminal records, children's data, health and biometric data such as voice) and transfers it abroad (Speechmatics, OpenRouter). That puts it in the DPIA, DPO and cross-border-transfer regime. The lawyer is most likely the controller and Jude a processor, or a controller for its own account and telemetry data.

### Cited Findings
- UU 27/2022 on PDP was enacted on 17 Oct 2022 with a 2-year transition, so it was fully binding from 17 Oct 2024. Pasal 4(2) lists "data pribadi yang bersifat spesifik": health data, biometric data, genetic data, **criminal records (catatan kejahatan)**, **children's data**, personal financial data, and others under the law. Pasal 56 covers cross-border transfer: equal or higher protection, otherwise adequate and binding safeguards, otherwise consent. Pasal 34 requires a DPIA for high-risk processing, including automated decisions with legal effect, specific data, large scale, and new technology. Pasal 46 requires breach notice within 3x24 hours. Pasal 15 contains exceptions for, among other things, "kepentingan proses penegakan hukum". **[unverified this session]** — [UU 27/2022, BPK](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/229798/uu-no-27-tahun-2022)
- PP 33/2026 (implementing regulation) was set and promulgated on 16 Jul 2026 (LNRI 2026 No. 88). Pasal 225 makes it effective six months later, on **16 Jan 2027**. Under Pasal 223, processing may continue until the agency issues its own rules, as long as it does not contradict the PP. — [matthewswong.com developer guide (secondary; author read a datahukum.com mirror)](https://www.matthewswong.com/en/blog/gr-33-2026-pdp-implementing-regulation-developers/); [SIP Law Firm](https://siplawfirm.id/id/publikasi/pp-33-2026-pelindungan-data-pribadi); [cybersecurity.or.id: "berlaku 16 Januari 2027"](https://www.cybersecurity.or.id/2026/10/pelindungan-data-pribadi-berlaku-16.html)
- PP 33/2026 cross-border transfer (Pasal 160–167) is tiered: (1) a country assessed as adequate by the agency (Pasal 167); (2) adequate and binding safeguards such as an assessed legal instrument plus supplementary measures (Pasal 161–162); (3) informed consent after disclosure of purpose, safeguards and risks (Pasal 164). — [matthewswong.com](https://www.matthewswong.com/en/blog/gr-33-2026-pdp-implementing-regulation-developers/)
- PP 33/2026 DPIA (Pasal 120–121): it must be completed before processing, have four minimum contents, and be reviewed when risk changes. The elucidation names AI and machine learning as "new technology". — [matthewswong.com](https://www.matthewswong.com/en/blog/gr-33-2026-pdp-implementing-regulation-developers/)
- PP 33/2026 DPO (Pasal 142–146) is required for, among other cases, large-scale processing of specific or **criminal-offence data**. The DPO may be external and must be independent. — [matthewswong.com](https://www.matthewswong.com/en/blog/gr-33-2026-pdp-implementing-regulation-developers/)
- PP 33/2026 on automated decisions (Pasal 93–96): data subjects may object to solely automated decisions with legal or significant effect. The controller must explain the technology and its consequences and offer human involvement. Breach notice stays at 3x24h (Pasal 114), and processors must notify controllers at the first opportunity (Pasal 118). Records of processing need 13 minimum fields (Pasal 74(2)). Rights requests (access, correction, restriction) must be handled within 3x24h. Fines are capped at 2% of annual revenue (Pasal 184–185). — [matthewswong.com](https://www.matthewswong.com/en/blog/gr-33-2026-pdp-implementing-regulation-developers/)
- Status of the PDP Agency: as of Sept 2026 it was not fully formed. It is to be created by Perpres, and Komdigi's Ditjen Pengawasan Ruang Digital handles matters in the interim. Adequacy assessments, SCC/BCR approval and the DPIA methodology all depend on the agency. — [matthewswong.com citing ABNR 15 Sep 2026](https://www.matthewswong.com/en/blog/gr-33-2026-pdp-implementing-regulation-developers/); [Kompas.id](https://www.kompas.id/artikel/peraturan-pelaksana-uu-pdp-akhirnya-terbit-lembaga-pengawas-masih-dinanti)
- Under PP 33/2026, the UU PDP criminal provisions can already be applied, but the technical procedures, including authorities' requests for personal data, await the PP's effective date and an operational agency. The controller definition covers "setiap orang", so individuals such as advocates can be controllers. — [Hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/pelindungan-data-pribadi-dalam-penegakan-hukum-dan-konfigurasi-beracara-di-bawah-pp-33-2026-lt6ac4acd9db1bd/)
- UU ITE (UU 11/2008 as amended by UU 19/2016 and UU 1/2024): Pasal 26 requires consent for using personal data in electronic media, and the law sets obligations for Electronic System Operators (PSE), including reliable and secure operation. Private-scope PSE must register with Komdigi under PP 71/2019 and Permenkominfo 5/2020. **[unverified this session]** — [UU 1/2024, BPK](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/274494/uu-no-1-tahun-2024)

### Inferences
- Jude's data mix includes case files with criminal records, witness and victim identities, possibly children's data and voice recordings (biometric). That is "specific" data under Pasal 4(2), so a **DPIA before launch** is effectively mandatory, and **DPO designation** is likely once Jude operates at scale.
- Streaming audio to Speechmatics and documents to OpenRouter and its upstream model providers is a **cross-border transfer**. No adequacy decisions exist yet because the agency is not operating. Realistic bases are binding contractual safeguards (DPAs, zero-retention and no-training terms) plus documented supplementary measures, with explicit informed consent as a fallback. Jude should keep a provider and region register and prefer providers with zero-data-retention and no-training guarantees. OpenRouter routes to many upstream providers, so the chain must be pinned and documented.
- Third parties (witnesses, victims, co-defendants, judges) are data subjects who never consented. The lawful basis will likely be legitimate interest or legal-obligation/defense grounds, possibly the law-enforcement exception (whether it covers private defense counsel is unclear). This should be documented in the DPIA.
- The 3x24h breach clock and 3x24h data-subject-rights SLA require incident runbooks and admin tooling.
- Findings are advisory and need human verification, so they are not "solely automated decisions with legal effect". Keeping it that way avoids the Pasal 93–96 objection regime, and the design should make it demonstrable.

### Gaps
- Official PP 33/2026 text was not fetched from JDIH or the State Gazette. Article numbers come from secondary readings.
- Whether advocates processing case data qualify for the UU PDP Pasal 15 law-enforcement or judicial-process exception was not found.
- The PSE registration obligation for a SaaS used by lawyers was not re-verified.

## Indonesia: advocate ethics and bar guidance on AI

### Takeaway
Confidentiality is statutory (UU Advokat Pasal 19) and ethical (KEAI Pasal 4 huruf h). On 6 Aug 2026 DPN Peradi launched a non-binding "Pedoman Penggunaan Kecerdasan Buatan (AI) bagi Advokat". It warns specifically against uploading client data to public AI services and may become the benchmark for judging professional conduct.

### Cited Findings
- UU 18/2003 on Advokat, Pasal 19(1): advocates must keep confidential everything learned from clients through the professional relationship, unless law provides otherwise. Pasal 19(2) protects files and documents from seizure and electronic communications from interception. — [Hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/hubungan-kerahasiaan-advokat-dan-klien-lt63b7e90220e9b/?page=all)
- Kode Etik Advokat Indonesia (23 May 2002), Pasal 4 huruf h: advocates must keep professional secrets told by clients in confidence, including after the relationship ends. — [Hukumonline Klinik](https://www.hukumonline.com/klinik/a/bolehkah-advokat-bersaksi-untuk-mantan-klien-lt5278b11b9573c/)
- DPN Peradi launched the "Pedoman Penggunaan Kecerdasan Buatan (AI) bagi Advokat" on Thursday 6 Aug 2026. Chair Ahmad Fikri Assegaf warned that uploading client data to public AI services is risky because there is "tidak ada jaminan bahwa data kerahasiaannya terjaga", and that clients would have a strong basis to sue. The guideline is not meant to restrict AI use. It focuses on legal reasoning quality, ethics and client confidentiality, and may become the "tolok ukur" for professional standards. The article does not detail provisions on verification, disclosure or prohibited uses. — [Hukumonline, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/peradi-luncurkan-pedoman-ai-bagi-advokat--ingatkan-etika-dan-perlindungan-data-klien-lt6a748379a7427/)
- The new KUHAP (UU 20/2025), effective January 2026, expands advocates' role. Advocates may accompany suspects, defendants, witnesses and victims at all stages (reported as Pasal 150) and may object to intimidating questions (reported as Pasal 32(2)). Suspect examinations must be CCTV-recorded, and the recordings can be used for the defense and at trial on the judge's request. One commentator says advocates may document or record during accompaniment. — [Hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/advokat-bukan-lagi-penonton-pasif-dalam-kuhap-baru-lt692a745cea9cf/); [Disway](https://jateng.disway.id/disway-solo/read/722831/kuhap-baru-wajibkan-pemeriksaan-tersangka-direkam-cctv); [Lintasindonews (secondary)](https://www.lintasindonews.com/kuhap-baru-ubah-peta-penegakan-hukum-pemeriksaan-tersangka-kini-wajib-direkam-rois-hidayat-advokat-harus-terus-belajar-dan-berani-mengawal-hak-klien/)

### Inferences
- Jude must not behave like a "public AI service". It needs enterprise contracts with no training on inputs and zero or minimal retention, encryption, per-case access control, and a clear statement of which third parties see the data, so that Peradi members can show compliance with Pasal 19 and KEAI 4(h).
- Client engagement letters should disclose AI processing and cross-border providers. This aligns with PDP consent and with ABA 512-style informed consent (see below). Jude can supply template disclosure language.
- Competence: the lawyer stays responsible for verifying every AI output. Jude's verified-quote design (findings tied to exact transcript or document spans) directly supports this.
- Multiple bar associations exist (Peradi, KAI, Peradi-RBA, Peradi-SAI, and others). The Peradi guideline binds only Peradi members ethically, but it is the most visible benchmark.

### Gaps
- The full text of the Peradi AI guideline was not found. Its specific provisions on verification, disclosure to courts or clients, and prohibited uses are unknown.
- No AI guidance from KAI or other bar associations was found.
- The exact UU 20/2025 article on advocates recording during accompaniment, and whether it extends to court hearings, was not verified in the official text.

## Indonesia: court rules on recording, closed hearings, and witness/victim/child protection

### Takeaway
Recording hearings requires the presiding judge's permission before the hearing starts (Perma 5/2020 Pasal 4(6)). Recording is not allowed at all in hearings closed to the public. Child cases (SPPA) and sexual-violence cases (TPKS) are tried in closed hearings, with criminal penalties for disclosing identities. Live transcription is therefore permission-gated and must be blocked or locked down by default in closed hearings.

### Cited Findings
- Perma 5/2020 on Protokol Persidangan dan Keamanan dalam Lingkungan Peradilan, promulgated 4 Dec 2020: Pasal 4(6) requires that photographing, audio recording or audio-visual recording have the permission of the judge or presiding judge before the hearing starts. Audio and audio-visual recording cannot be done in hearings closed to the public. The stated purpose is orderly hearings. It was amended by Perma 6/2020. — [BPK: Perma 5/2020](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/206077/perma-no-5-tahun-2020); [BPK: Perma 6/2020](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/206078/perma-no-6-tahun-2020); [Kumparan](https://kumparan.com/kumparannews/ambil-foto-saat-sidang-harus-izin-hakim-ma-tegaskan-bukan-batasi-kerja-jurnalis-1uoe4YFhyWT); [Hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/melihat-pedoman-protokol-dan-pengamanan-persidangan-lt5fdc57afbee7d/)
- UU 11/2012 SPPA: Pasal 19 requires the identity of child offenders, child victims and child witnesses (name, parents' names, address, face, anything identifying) to be kept confidential in print and electronic media. Pasal 54 makes child hearings closed to the public, except the reading of the verdict. Pasal 97 provides up to 5 years' imprisonment for breaching Pasal 19(1). Sources give different fine amounts (Rp500 million vs Rp200 million). — [Hukumonline Klinik](https://www.hukumonline.com/klinik/a/identitas-anak-korban-tindak-pidana-wajib-dirahasiakan-ini-sanksi-jika-dilanggar-lt61640c948790b); [Hukumonline Klinik on closed hearings](https://www.hukumonline.com/klinik/a/persidangan-perkara-anak-tidak-selalu-tertutup-untuk-umum-lt5c75daddb3abf/); [KPAI](https://kpai.go.id/?p=12091)
- UU 12/2022 TPKS: cases are examined in closed hearings, the verdict is read publicly, and the judge must keep the identities of witnesses and victims confidential. One secondary source reports up to 5 years' imprisonment for disclosing victim or witness identity. — [Hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/cara-melaporkan-kasus-kekerasan-dan-pelecehan-seksual-lt64db5ae59fe76?page=2); [Wartaekonomi (single source on penalty)](https://wartaekonomi.co.id/read430000/perkuat-implementasikan-uu-tpks-kementerian-pppa-lakukan-hal-ini)
- UU 31/2014 (amending UU 13/2006) on Witness and Victim Protection: protected witnesses and victims have rights including identity secrecy and testimony without facing the defendant, with protection granted by LPSK (Pasal 5). **[unverified this session]** — [UU 31/2014, BPK](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/38716/uu-no-31-tahun-2014)

### Inferences
- **Recording consent gate:** before any capture, Jude should require the user to confirm and record that the presiding judge granted permission (who, when, scope). The permission should be stored in the audit trail, and capture blocked without it.
- **Closed-hearing mode:** if a hearing is flagged as SPPA, TPKS or otherwise closed, audio capture should be disabled by default (Perma 4(6)). If a court nonetheless allows note-taking or transcription, identities should be redacted or pseudonymized, the data processed in-country or only with strict providers, and export or sharing locked.
- **Identity protection:** child and TPKS victim or witness identities must never appear in exports, shared `.jude.zip` bundles, rehearsal content, or LLM prompts unless strictly necessary. Pseudonymization before sending text to OpenRouter is a strong design choice.
- LPSK-protected witnesses need a "protected identity" flag that propagates to redaction.

### Gaps
- The exact wording of Perma 6/2020 changes to Pasal 4 was not verified.
- No MA rule specifically addressing live machine transcription by parties, as distinct from "recording", was found. Whether streaming audio to a cloud ASR counts as "rekaman audio" is untested, but conservatively it should be treated as recording.
- Exact UU TPKS article numbers for closed hearings and identity penalties were not verified in the official text.

## Indonesia: national AI governance and Mahkamah Agung AI policy

### Takeaway
There is no binding Indonesian AI law yet. SE Menkominfo 9/2023 (AI ethics, soft law) applies now. Two Perpres (AI ethics with risk tiers, and the National AI Roadmap 2026–2029) were still "in the signing process" on 30 Oct 2026. MA has issued **no** PERMA or SEMA on AI as of Nov 2026. It publicly states that AI does not decide cases, runs AI training (with a "VERIFY" protocol), and in Oct 2026 said it was considering generative-AI guidance.

### Cited Findings
- Surat Edaran Menkominfo No. 9/2023 on AI Ethics (Dec 2023) sets non-binding values for AI developers and users: inclusivity, humanity, security, accessibility, transparency, credibility and accountability, personal data protection, and sustainability. It also says AI must not be the policy or decision maker for humans. **[unverified this session]** — [JDIH Komdigi](https://jdih.komdigi.go.id/produk_hukum/view/id/883/t/surat+edaran+menteri+komunikasi+dan+informatika+nomor+9+tahun+2023)
- Draft AI-ethics Perpres: as of 30 Oct 2026 it was still being signed, per Said Mirza Pahlevi (Komdigi Director of AI). It is risk-based. Prohibited AI includes AI that endangers safety or citizens' rights, such as mass public facial recognition without legal basis. High-risk AI is allowed under special supervision with sectoral requirements. Final decisions must not be fully automated, and humans decide, especially for high-risk AI. Other obligations: AI-output labeling, transparency on data processing, data-deletion duties, and complaint handling. Businesses self-assess with no new licence, prototypes are exempt until commercial use, and there is a 2-year adjustment period. The article does not mention justice or law enforcement. — [CNBC Indonesia, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/tech/20261030162941-37-781009/ri-atur-ai-sesuai-risiko-komdigi-beberkan-isi-regulasinya)
- Inter-ministry discussion of both draft Perpres finished in May 2026. In July 2026 they were awaiting presidential signature. Komdigi calls them a step toward an AI law. — [Bisnis.com, 16 Jul 2026](https://teknologi.bisnis.com/read/20260716/84/1988624/peta-jalan-dan-etika-ai-nasional-masih-diproses-menuju-perpres); [Tempo](https://www.tempo.co/politik/komdigi-masih-tunggu-peta-jalan-dan-perpres-etika-ai-2276220); [Komdigi press release](https://www.komdigi.go.id/berita/siaran-pers/detail/kemkomdigi-jadikan-perpres-ai-sebagai-langkah-awal-menuju-undang-undang-ai); [Katadata](https://katadata.co.id/digital/teknologi/69df0e8039515/dua-perpres-ai-segera-terbit-atur-tiga-tingkatan-risiko-dan-mitigasi-kerugian)
- The National AI Roadmap covers 2026–2029, targets 11 strategic sectors, and aims for 45,000 AI talents per year. — [Infobank](https://infobanknews.com/komdigi-siapkan-peta-jalan-ai-2026-2029-begini-progresnya); [CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/tech/20261030162941-37-781009/ri-atur-ai-sesuai-risiko-komdigi-beberkan-isi-regulasinya)
- MA: the only 2025 SEMA (SEMA 1/2025, 30 Dec 2025) adopts chamber plenary formulations and is not about AI. — [JDIH MA](https://jdih.mahkamahagung.go.id/legal-product/sema-nomor-1-tahun-2025/detail); [Kepaniteraan MA](https://kepaniteraan.mahkamahagung.go.id/id/registry-news/2856-sah-ma-berlakukan-24-rumusan-kamar-hasil-pleno-kamar-2025-sebagai-pedoman-pelaksanaan-tugas-pengadilan)
- MA statements: AI cannot replace judicial independence and conscience. MA states AI is not a decision maker and judges retain control (Oct 2026). MA held AI training for court officials on 21–25 Sep 2026, and a second wave in late Oct 2026 introduced a "VERIFY" protocol to check AI output before use. In Oct 2026 an official said MA is considering drafting generative-AI guidance. Commentators propose principles, permitted-use zones, a ban on uploading case files to public AI, and verification duties. — [MariNews](https://marinews.mahkamahagung.go.id/berita/ai-tidak-bisa-menggantikan-independensi-dan-nurani-0b3l); [Monitorpos, Oct 2026](https://www.monitorpos.net/2026/10/ma-ri-tegaskan-ai-bukan-pengambil.html); [Sudutpandang](https://sudutpandang.id/ma-gelar-pelatihan-ai-untuk-aparatur-peradilan/); [Dandapala](https://dandapala.com/article/detail/menjaga-independensi-hakim-di-era-ai-batas-etika-penggunaan-ai-dalam-peradilan)

### Inferences
- Jude should be built to the likely Perpres shape now: human-final decisions, AI-output labeling (every finding or rehearsal line visibly marked as AI-generated), transparency about data use, deletion capability, a complaint channel, and a documented self-assessment of risk tier. Litigation support touching liberty interests would plausibly be "high risk" if a sector regulator (MA or Kemenkum) adds requirements.
- MA's emerging stance (verification, no uploading case files to public AI) mirrors what courts may later expect of lawyers' AI-assisted submissions. Jude's verified-quote approach and exportable provenance should anticipate a future duty to disclose or verify AI use.

### Gaps
- The full text of the draft Perpres was not available. It is unknown whether justice or legal services are listed as high risk.
- SE 9/2023 content was not re-fetched this session.
- The content of MA's "VERIFY" protocol was not obtained.

## Global guidance (ABA 512, UK Judiciary, CEPEJ, EU AI Act, UNESCO, standing orders)

### Takeaway
Global guidance converges on six duties: competence and understanding of the tool, confidentiality and informed consent before entering client data, independent verification of every output (hallucinated cases and quotes are the signature failure), human accountability, transparency or disclosure, and bias awareness. Under the EU AI Act, "administration of justice" high-risk status (Annex III 8(a)) targets tools used by or on behalf of judicial authorities. Jude, as a defense-lawyer tool, likely falls outside it, but it is the best available design benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- **ABA Formal Opinion 512** (29 Jul 2024) covers generative AI. Under Model Rule 1.1 competence, lawyers need a reasonable understanding of the tool's capabilities and limits and must independently verify output. Under Rule 1.6, they need the client's *informed* consent before entering client information into self-learning tools, and boilerplate in engagement letters is insufficient. Rule 1.4 requires communication with clients about AI use in some circumstances. Rules 3.1, 3.3 and 8.4(c) on candor require checking citations and quotes. Rules 5.1 and 5.3 require supervision of staff and vendors. Rule 1.5 requires reasonable fees, so time saved cannot be billed as if spent. **[unverified this session]** — [ABA Formal Opinion 512](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/ethics-opinions/aba-formal-opinion-512.pdf)
- **UK Judiciary AI Guidance** (updated Oct 2025, replacing April 2025 and Dec 2023 versions; the PDF is dated 8 Oct 2025, others say 31 Oct): treat public AI tools as capable of making anything entered into them public. AI may fabricate cases and quotes. Judges must read the underlying documents, because AI cannot replace engagement with evidence. A separate Position Statement on judicial use of AI (reported 9 Oct 2026) requires every AI output used in judicial work to be reviewed, verified and approved by an accountable office holder. Approved tools reportedly keep data in the UK, use enterprise data protection, and disable web access. — [judiciary.uk guidance](https://www.judiciary.uk/guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence-ai-judicial-guidance/); [PDF](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Artificial-Intelligence-AI-Guidance-for-Judicial-Office-Holders-1.pdf); [Position statement](https://www.judiciary.uk/guidance-and-resources/position-statement-on-the-judicial-use-of-artificial-intelligence-ai/); [HSF Kramer, Oct 2026](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/litigation/2026-10/new-position-statement-on-judicial-use-of-ai)
- **CEPEJ European Ethical Charter on the use of AI in judicial systems** (adopted 3–4 Dec 2018) has five principles: (1) respect for fundamental rights, including fair trial and equality of arms; (2) non-discrimination; (3) quality and security, meaning certified sources and secure environments; (4) transparency, impartiality and fairness, meaning accessible and understandable methods and external audits; (5) "under user control", meaning users stay informed actors, not prescriptive outputs. **[unverified this session]** — [Council of Europe CEPEJ Charter](https://rm.coe.int/ethical-charter-en-for-publication-4-december-2018/16808f699c)
- **EU AI Act** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Annex III point 8(a), lists as high-risk "AI systems intended to be used by a judicial authority or on their behalf to assist a judicial authority in researching and interpreting facts and the law and in applying the law to a concrete set of facts, or to be used in a similar way in alternative dispute resolution." Recital 61 excludes purely ancillary administrative activities. High-risk obligations include risk management, data governance and bias examination, logging, transparency, human oversight (Art 14), accuracy and robustness, and for deployers awareness of automation bias (Art 14(4)(b)). **[unverified this session]** — [EUR-Lex 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- **EU Digital Omnibus on AI**, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (reported published in OJ 24 Jul 2026, in force 27 Jul 2026), postponed Annex III high-risk obligations to **2 Dec 2027**, Annex I to 2 Aug 2028, and reportedly to 2 Aug 2030 for some public-authority systems. The Art 50(2) synthetic-content marking moved to 2 Dec 2026. Sources conflict on the Council date (13 May vs 29 Jun 2026). — [ActuIA](https://www.actuia.com/en/news/ai-act-obligations-in-force-and-delays-under-omnibus-2026-1744/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/digital-ai-omnibus-delays-key-deadlines-introduces-new-rules/)
- **UNESCO Guidelines for the Use of AI Systems in Courts and Tribunals**: the draft was published in 2024 with 13 principles (consultation until 5 Sep 2024). The final version (Dec 2025, a "living document") reportedly has 15 principles. Draft principles include proportionality, safety, information security, awareness and informed use, transparent use, accountability and auditability, explainability, accuracy and reliability, human oversight, and human-centric design. AI is a supportive tool, not a substitute for legal reasoning. A UNESCO survey found 44% of judicial operators use AI but only 9% have had training. — [Burges Salmon (draft)](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102jgyt/unesco-guidelines-for-use-of-ai-systems-in-courts-and-tribunals-draft-published/); [UN Innovation Network](https://www.uninnovation.network/innovation-library/guidelines-for-the-use-of-ai-systems-in-courts-and-tribunals); [Odisha Plus, Dec 2025](https://odisha.plus/2025/12/unesco-global-ai-guidelines-for-judicial-systems/); [Marval](https://www.marval.com/Publicacion/pautas-para-el-uso-de-ia-en-tribunales-17492?lang=en)
- **Court standing orders on AI disclosure**: for example, Judge Brantley Starr (N.D. Tex., May 2023) requires certification that no generative AI was used, or that its output was human-verified. This followed *Mata v. Avianca* (S.D.N.Y. 2023), where lawyers were sanctioned for ChatGPT-fabricated citations. **[unverified this session]** — [N.D. Tex. Judge Starr requirements](https://www.txnd.uscourts.gov/judge/judge-brantley-starr); [Mata v. Avianca sanctions order](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1:2022cv01461/575368/54/)

### Inferences
- Jude is used by private defense counsel, not "by or on behalf of a judicial authority", so it is likely outside EU Annex III 8(a). It has no EU market anyway. Even so, aligning with Art 12 logging, Art 13 transparency and Art 14 human oversight is a credible "best practice" claim.
- ABA 512's informed-consent standard (specific, not boilerplate) is a stricter template that fits UU PDP consent and Peradi's confidentiality emphasis. Jude should offer client-consent text naming Speechmatics, OpenRouter and the upstream models, the countries involved, retention, and no-training.
- The failure mode every source highlights is fabricated quotes. Jude's design of accepting only findings whose quotes verify verbatim against the source transcript or document is directly responsive, and should be preserved and surfaced.
- CEPEJ's "equality of arms" cuts in Jude's favor (it helps under-resourced defense), but its "under user control" principle requires that outputs be non-prescriptive.

### Gaps
- The official UNESCO final text and exact 15-principle list were not retrieved, so the principle names are from secondary sources.
- ABA 512, the CEPEJ Charter, EU AI Act article text and standing orders were not re-fetched this session (recorded from known primary URLs).

## Bias, automation bias, explainability, audit trails and human-in-the-loop

### Takeaway
Commercial ASR shows large accuracy gaps across speaker groups (WER 0.35 vs 0.19 in Koenecke et al. 2020). Indonesian courtrooms involve regional accents, code-switching (Javanese, Sundanese, Batak, Minang, and others), interpreters and poor acoustics, so transcript errors can create false "inconsistencies" that disproportionately affect certain witnesses. Mitigations: show ASR confidence, keep audio linked to every quote, require human confirmation, log everything, and never label a witness as non-credible.

### Cited Findings
- Koenecke et al., "Racial disparities in automated speech recognition", PNAS 117(14):7684–7689 (2020), tested five commercial ASR systems (Amazon, Apple, Google, IBM, Microsoft). Average WER was 0.35 for Black speakers vs 0.19 for white speakers, and the gap persisted when speakers were matched on the same phrases. The authors attribute it to acoustic-model training data. — [Paper PDF](https://5harad.com/papers/asr-disparities.pdf); [Stanford News](https://news.stanford.edu/?p=32685); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v117y2020p7684-7689.html)
- The UK judicial guidance warns that AI output may be inaccurate, incomplete or biased and can fabricate cases and quotes, and that judges remain personally responsible. — [judiciary.uk PDF](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Artificial-Intelligence-AI-Guidance-for-Judicial-Office-Holders-1.pdf); [UK Human Rights Blog](https://ukhumanrightsblog.com/2025/12/03/the-latest-judicial-guidance-on-ai-white-text-bias-fakes-hallucinations-the-use-of-ai-by-litigants-in-person-and-lawyers/)
- The EU AI Act Art 14(4)(b) requires high-risk systems to enable overseers to "remain aware of the possible tendency of automatically relying or over-relying on the output" (automation bias). Art 12 requires automatic event logging. **[unverified this session]** — [EUR-Lex 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- UNESCO lists accountability and auditability, explainability, and human oversight. CEPEJ requires transparency and external audit and that users stay in control. — [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102jgyt/unesco-guidelines-for-use-of-ai-systems-in-courts-and-tribunals-draft-published/); [CEPEJ Charter](https://rm.coe.int/ethical-charter-en-for-publication-4-december-2018/16808f699c)
- Indonesia's draft AI Perpres and MA statements both require that final decisions remain human. — [CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/tech/20261030162941-37-781009/ri-atur-ai-sesuai-risiko-komdigi-beberkan-isi-regulasinya); [Monitorpos](https://www.monitorpos.net/2026/10/ma-ri-tegaskan-ai-bukan-pengambil.html)

### Inferences (consolidated design requirements for Jude)
1. **Recording authorization gate**: capture requires confirming the presiding judge's permission (Perma 5/2020 Pasal 4(6)), logged with timestamp and user. Capture is disabled by default for closed hearings (SPPA, TPKS, decency cases).
2. **Protected-identity handling**: flags for child, TPKS victim and LPSK-protected persons. Their names are pseudonymized before LLM calls and redacted in exports and `.jude.zip`. Criminal sanctions apply under SPPA Pasal 97 and UU TPKS.
3. **PDP compliance pack**: a DPIA before launch (specific data plus new AI technology), a records-of-processing register (13 fields), DPO designation, a 3x24h breach runbook, 3x24h data-subject-rights handling, retention and deletion controls, and preparation for PP 33/2026 taking effect on 16 Jan 2027.
4. **Cross-border provider governance**: DPAs with Speechmatics, OpenRouter and the upstream models; zero data retention and no training; pinned providers and regions; a documented transfer basis (PP 33/2026 Pasal 160–167) with consent fallback. Disclose this to lawyers and their clients.
5. **Client informed-consent template** (ABA 512 standard, Peradi confidentiality, PDP consent): name the providers, countries, retention period, and the human-verification workflow.
6. **Verified provenance for every finding**: findings must quote verbatim, link to the source span (transcript segment with audio timestamp, or document page), and be rejected when the quote does not verify. This addresses the hallucinated-quote failure that every guidance document highlights.
7. **ASR uncertainty surfaced**: per-segment confidence, a "possible transcription error" label, one-click audio playback for any quoted segment, speaker-diarization caveats, and interpreter or dialect markers. Low-confidence segments cannot alone ground an "inconsistency".
8. **Non-determinative language**: "potential inconsistency for counsel's review", never "lie", "not credible" or a guilt likelihood. No credibility or guilt scores, in line with CEPEJ "under user control", the Perpres human-final rule, and MA's stance.
9. **Automation-bias countermeasures**: require explicit accept or dismiss with a reason, show counter-evidence and alternative explanations (translation, ASR error, ambiguity), and avoid ranking that implies certainty.
10. **Audit trail**: immutable logs of recordings, permissions, model and provider and version, prompts and inputs (hashed or redacted), outputs, user verification actions, and exports. This supports EU Art 12-style logging, UNESCO auditability, and potential disciplinary or court scrutiny.
11. **AI labeling**: every AI-generated artifact (findings, rehearsal scripts, summaries) is visibly labeled, per the draft Perpres labeling duty and the EU Art 50 trend.
12. **Rehearsal safeguards**: AI trial rehearsal must be clearly simulated, must not coach witnesses to give false testimony (advocate ethics and obstruction risk), and must not be presented as a prediction of judicial outcomes.
13. **Court-disclosure readiness**: if Indonesian courts adopt AI-disclosure rules, as US standing orders do, lawyers should be able to export a provenance report showing which content was AI-assisted and human-verified.

### Gaps
- No published ASR accuracy benchmarks for Indonesian regional accents, code-switched speech or courtroom acoustics (including Speechmatics' Indonesian model) were found.
- No empirical studies on automation bias among Indonesian lawyers or judges were found.
- No Indonesian case law on admissibility or ethics of AI-generated transcripts or analyses was found.
