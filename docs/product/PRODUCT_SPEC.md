# Jude product spec

## Problem and users

Lawyers in Indonesian criminal proceedings must follow testimony while comparing investigation documents, statements, and legal references. Jude keeps testimony, qualified findings, and inspectable evidence together. Lawyers retain judgment; the system does not determine guilt, credibility, truthfulness, or legal outcomes.

## Delivered MVP

The marketing introduction remains at `/`, with a prominent Open workspace action and Sign in links in desktop/mobile navigation. `/workspace` provides account registration/sign-in, isolated cases, document ingestion/search/original download, transcript capture, progressive source-linked findings, independent-review details, review notes, speaker labels, legal discovery, provider status, portable `.jude` export/import, a separate hearing library, and an evidence graph with natural-language search.

Local SQLite and private files work without external accounts. Optional PostgreSQL/pgvector and private S3 storage paths are supplied. Amazon Transcribe streaming, Amazon Bedrock, S3, Pasal.id and the optional voice service are implemented with protocol/contract tests, and OpenRouter remains an optional local AI provider. Real provider validation was done in production on 2026-11-02: live microphone transcription through Amazon Transcribe streaming and Bedrock screening, review, assistant and practice calls (on the public demo case) were verified. Pasal.id, production S3 and the voice service have not yet had an authorized live acceptance run. Pasal.id responses remain discovery metadata, separate from case evidence.

## AI models (Bedrock)

The default tier (`BEDROCK_MODEL`, statement screening, case assistant, practice and transcript tasks) is Amazon Nova 2 Lite (`global.amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0`). The review tier (`BEDROCK_REVIEW_MODEL`, the independent check of every potential inconsistency) is Claude Sonnet 4.6 (`global.anthropic.claude-sonnet-4-6`), a different model family. Both are global cross-Region inference profiles. The choice, the benchmark on the public demo case and the prices are recorded in [`docs/phases/2026-11-02-model-choice.md`](../phases/2026-11-02-model-choice.md); `apps/web/scripts/benchmark-models.ts` and `benchmark-chat.ts` rerun it. Structured Bedrock calls get one retry when the tool call misses a required field (the retry names only the field paths), nested arrays sent as JSON strings are parsed, `temperature` is omitted for models that reject it, and throttled calls back off with adaptive SDK retries. Quotes are matched to the source with whitespace and straight or curly quotation marks treated as equivalent. Practice replies cannot use the user's own role (enforced in the tool schema).

The scripted synthetic hearing (warehouse fixture and simulation endpoints) was removed on 2026-11-02, and its remaining UI, runtime state and copy on 2026-11-02 ([cleanup record](../phases/2026-11-02-cleanup.md)). Stored rows of kind or mode `simulation` stay readable and render as ordinary hearings and findings. The only demonstration case is the public record below. Manual testimony without a configured AI provider is saved with an explicit unavailable state.

The fictional Cendana warehouse pack remains under `examples/` for upload practice only.

## Court decisions from the Direktori Putusan MA

Documents may be up to 25 MB, 1,500 PDF pages and 3,000,000 extracted characters (after cleanup). PDF text items are joined by position, so words split across font runs are rejoined. When a document carries Direktori Putusan MA markers, the rotated watermark letters, the `putusan.mahkamahagung.go.id` header, "Hal. X dari Y hal. Putusan Nomor ..." lines and the per-page disclaimer are removed from the extracted text; the stored original is unchanged and quotations are verified against the cleaned passages. The full 696-page decision 796/Pid.B/2022/PN Jkt.Sel ingests in about 2 seconds into 1,812 passages (peak memory about 0.6 GB in a local run). Hearing uploads remain limited to 6 MB.

## Hearing and evidence workspace

Hearings are records within a case, each with a title, date, transcript, findings, and audio session. The hearing library searches titles, dates, and transcript phrases and can show only hearings with unreviewed findings. Within a hearing, literal text and speaker filters highlight matching testimony and suspend auto-follow without changing the record. Review counts and a next-unreviewed action support systematic review; unsaved notes remain explicit. Uploading a transcript opens a review preview before mutation. Native parsers cover the Jude hearing schema, CSV, SRT, VTT, and speaker-turn transcripts as they are published, in TXT, Markdown, text PDF or DOCX: official risalah (numbered `ROLE: NAME [mm:ss]` turns with front matter and page noise), court-style `HAKIM KETUA:` records with wrapped lines, timestamped notes and one-line `Speaker: text` files. The dominant turn marker is detected per document; text stays verbatim. Verified on the Mahkamah Konstitusi risalah for Perkara 115/PUU-XXII/2024 (112 turns, six named speakers). Text-based PDFs/DOCX and unfamiliar readable text can be converted explicitly with the configured AI provider; output is rejected if it invents wording. Uncertain speaker identities become Unknown; AI conversion supplies no invented timestamps. Original files are preserved. Analysis excludes that hearing's uploaded source document to prevent self-corroboration.

The evidence map connects source passages and cited hearing statements. Semantic similarity supplies candidates, never a conclusion. Optional bounded LLM classification attaches exact quotations to suggested relationships; users can confirm or manually create links. Search retrieves case-isolated evidence and expands through confirmed links only; the answer model must cite exact retrieved passages. Citation matching verifies provenance, not the truth of an interpretation.

`.jude` version 1 is a bounded ZIP with a JSON manifest. Case archives carry originals, sources, hearings, transcript, findings, notes, and evidence links. Import always creates a new case with remapped IDs. Imported findings and links require renewed review; external legal metadata and embedding vectors are excluded from re-use. See [JUDE_FORMAT.md](../engineering/JUDE_FORMAT.md).

## Case assistant and courtroom practice

**Case assistant** is a persistent conversation within one case. Its context includes case metadata, the document/hearing inventory, recent qualified findings, relevant testimony, retrieved source passages, and confirmed evidence connections. Users can pin up to six document/hearing references or open an exact passage/statement from the evidence inspector. Follow-ups retain recent conversation and source references. The model receives selected excerpts, not every page: a maximum of 20 excerpts at 2,400 characters, recent 12 messages, 40 findings, and 8 relevant connections. Each response exposes the actual source-selection limits. Other cases and practice conversations never enter retrieval.

Case quotations are checked against the supplied text before an exchange is saved. Whitespace-only differences from PDF/DOCX extraction are mapped back to the exact source substring; words, punctuation, numbers and source IDs must still match. Assistant replies and practice debriefs get at most one automatic correction attempt after a citation failure, using the same selected sources. A failed correction saves neither the draft nor the user exchange; it cannot bypass required citations by changing the case-facts flag. This can add one model call on failure; provider/network errors are not retried by this correction step. These checks establish provenance, not semantic correctness; readers can expand quotations and open original documents or hearings. Optional Pasal.id lookup contributes labeled discovery metadata, never invented statute text. Uploaded legal documents can be referenced like other case material. A missing AI provider produces an explicit sources-only response; missing Pasal disables external lookup. Provider failures preserve the draft, reject partial exchanges, and support idempotent retries.

**Courtroom practice** is an AI rehearsal in a separate saved conversation. The user picks a role (Penasihat Hukum, Penuntut Umum or Hakim Ketua), optionally reviews the conditions (trial type, jurisdiction/court, stage, difficulty, language, goals, assumptions), and starts. An AI provider can propose conditions from the current case and every field stays editable. Saved conditions stay stable during a rehearsal, and editing them starts a new one. The AI plays the other people in the room by role: for defense counsel the Hakim Ketua, the JPU and a witness, for the prosecutor the bench, defense counsel and a witness, for the judge both counsel and a witness. Speeches use Indonesian courtroom register (Yang Mulia, Saudara Saksi, "Keberatan, Yang Mulia") in the chosen language, follow KUHAP practice for the selected stage, and adjust objections, bench strictness and witness cooperativeness to the difficulty. The AI can never speak the user's role, and speeches under any other label are dropped.

Each turn retrieves case passages and testimony like the assistant (including referenced material). A witness is drawn from the record, named only as the sources name them, may only state what the cited material records, and answers "Saya tidak tahu/tidak ingat" outside it. Every case fact cites an exact passage checked before saving. One correction runs on a citation failure, after which a speech that still fails is dropped (never saved uncited), and the exchange fails without saving only if no verified speech remains. After each turn an optional coaching note (leading question, compound question, contradiction to press with a cited passage, procedure, clarity, or what worked) appears as a small aside, and an unverifiable note is dropped. Coaching can be switched off per viewer. Finish produces a debrief saved as one message per item: a summary, what worked, what to tighten with better phrasings, and up to four passages to revisit, each citing one verified passage. Practice never scores anyone, judges honesty, credibility or guilt, or predicts outcomes. Generated text is asked to avoid em dashes and mid-sentence semicolons.

Practice requires an AI provider. Without one the tab shows a calm unavailable state and the API returns 503, with no scripted substitute. The guided no-AI drill was removed on 2026-11-02. Its saved sessions stay readable and exportable but cannot continue. With Bedrock the default model (Nova Lite) runs turns in about 2 to 3 seconds and a debrief in 8 to 20 seconds. Observed limits: occasional misordered objection, ruling and answer, coaching notes sometimes in English, and debrief points that can mention questions not asked (their cited passages still verify). Practice never creates testimony, findings, or evidence links. Conversations are owner-checked and persisted separately, with up to 60 sessions per case and a bounded exchange history (roughly 80 messages). One exchange may run per conversation at a time. Separate JSON exports preserve assistant/practice history; `.jude` version 1 archives continue to carry the evidentiary case record and exclude dialogue.

## Recurring voices

Amazon Transcribe speaker labels (`spk_0`, `spk_1`) last for one streaming connection, so without help every new connection and every hearing shows new labels. With the optional Python voice service configured, each finalized recorded statement of at least 3 seconds is matched to the case's voiceprints. Recurring voices keep one key (`Voice 1`, `Voice 2`) across connections and hearings, so a label such as "Hakim Ketua" is entered once. A match is a suggestion that two clips probably share a voice; Jude never names a person, and users confirm labels. Short statements keep the provider label until their speaker is identified. Voiceprints are biometric data: they stay per case, are excluded from `.jude` archives, never leave the server except to the configured voice service, and can be deleted with "Forget voiceprints". Without the service, labels remain per connection.

## Languages

The whole interface (marketing page, sign-in, workspace, empty states, loading labels, accessible names, placeholders and confirmations) is available in Bahasa Indonesia, the primary language, and English. A visible "ID | EN" toggle sits in the landing header, the sign-in panel and the workspace top bar. The choice is saved in a `lang` cookie (so the server renders the right language and `<html lang>` on first paint) and in localStorage; without a saved choice the browser's `Accept-Language`/`navigator.language` decides (`id*` → Indonesian, any other language → English, no signal → Indonesian). Dates and numbers use `Intl` with `id-ID` or `en-US`. Indonesian copy uses KUHAP terms (Keterangan Saksi, BAP, Penuntut Umum, Penasihat Hukum, Majelis Hakim/Hakim Ketua, Terdakwa, Berkas Perkara) and keeps the finding statuses descriptive: Didukung sumber, Berbeda dari sumber, Perlu konteks, Bukti belum cukup. The brand stays "Jude".

AI analysis and the assistant answer in the language of the testimony. Courtroom practice defaults its editable `language` condition (and other free-text defaults) to the interface language; API contracts are unchanged. Server `detail` messages stay English; the browser shows the common ones (sign-in, rate limits, not found, upload type, busy operations, generic failures) in the interface language. Server-generated text such as graph/import notices, legal metadata, the demo case content and model output remains as produced.

## Loading behavior

Layout-matched skeletons cover workspace startup, case lists/records, evidence maps/search, document uploads/readers/search, hearing import previews/conversion, original finding passages, conversation history/opening/replies, practice suggestions/startup, legal search and save/import/audio preparation feedback. Initial data loads no longer show premature empty states. Existing records remain visible during background refreshes; explicit request failures end loading and preserve errors and retry controls. Skeletons expose named status regions, honor reduced motion, and pause animation when hidden or offscreen. The marketing page stays immediately readable without artificial loading delays.

## Constraints and decisions

Local and production modes both run one server process with bounded in-process analysis tasks; production uses PostgreSQL for data, leases, rate limits and coalesced events. Migrations run before the server starts, never inside it. Sessions are application-owned. Backend ownership checks protect every case route. No OCR, native courtroom recording, account recovery, multi-user case sharing, automatic naming of speakers, or operational prosecutor/judge workflow (practice roles are supported). Configured providers receive relevant material; no unverified privacy, accuracy, latency, customer, or commercial claims.

Original brief: “Jude - Evidence-Grounded AI for Courtroom Intelligence.” The portable scope and implementation map are recorded in [`docs/phases/2026-10-30-product-handoff.md`](../phases/2026-10-30-product-handoff.md); its initial failure list is superseded by the completion record.


## Deployment behavior

The native Svelte 5 frontend and TypeScript backend run as one SvelteKit 3 Node server (`@sveltejs/adapter-node`) built from `apps/web`. Production runs it in Docker on one AWS EC2 instance with PostgreSQL + pgvector, case originals in a private S3 bucket (`S3_BUCKET`) and AI through Amazon Bedrock (`AI_PROVIDER=bedrock`); see [`infra/README.md`](../../infra/README.md). OpenRouter remains an optional alternative AI provider.

Saved testimony is committed before its analysis is queued in the same process. At most three statements analyze at once and duplicate retries coalesce; a full queue or provider failure leaves the statement saved and retryable. Expired audio sessions and temporary coordination rows are cleaned opportunistically when cases load. Cookie-authenticated SSE supplies database-backed update notifications while the UI polls authoritative records.

The browser streams PCM audio directly to Amazon Transcribe streaming over a WebSocket URL presigned by the server (valid for five minutes to connect). The server's AWS credentials never leave it. Finalized batches are authenticated, validated and saved idempotently through the TypeScript API. One Transcribe stream may last four hours, so the browser reconnects with a fresh presigned URL after 3 h 50 min, flushing final buffers and bounding reconnect buffering. Failures remain explicit. Speaker identifiers are scoped to each provider connection and timeline offsets continue across connections. After the user confirms that the presiding judge allowed recording, the browser also records the session with MediaRecorder (Opus at 32 kbps where supported) and uploads it when capture stops. Recordings are stored privately (S3 in production), replay with HTTP Range seeking, stay per case and can be deleted. Partials remain local to the recording browser. Uploads are single bounded multipart requests, and originals and exports download directly from the API.

The previous Python runtime and test harness have been replaced with TypeScript. Existing PostgreSQL data, password hashes, originals and `.jude` version 1 remain compatible; migration 006 adds browser-audio session state. These paths have local/mocked contract checks and a separate PostgreSQL suite. Transcribe and Bedrock were verified in production on 2026-11-02. Pasal.id, S3 and the voice service still need an authorized live acceptance run.

## Everyday workspace usability

Normal screens describe the task, available actions and relevant limits. Deployment setup, credential instructions, storage configuration, embedding counts and retrieval diagnostics are kept out of the workspace. Provider failures retain an opaque support reference; service/status/configuration diagnostics remain in server logs. Unavailable features explain an alternative without implying they are working.

Empty cases offer direct paths to documents and hearings and explain how these feed Evidence. Case navigation supports title/reference search. Document uploads confirm completion and searches can be cleared. Speaker labels replace the former connections panel. Audio capture is available above the transcript, with a disclosure for existing hearing records. Saved conversations collapse on small screens. Sign-in supports password visibility and clears stale errors when switching to registration.

The original landing composition now includes a finite, replayable comparison sequence: the two quoted times highlight, the source connection draws, and the finding receives emphasis. Introductory and workflow motion stays bounded, pauses offscreen/when hidden, and respects reduced motion. The page remains readable without JavaScript. The "Look closer" demo section shows three verbatim excerpts from the public demo case (one supported, one context needed, one insufficient evidence), each pairing a line of the automatic hearing transcript with the putusan's summary of testimony, labeled as a public record. The hero comparison remains a labeled synthetic illustration.
